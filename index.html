The Definition and The Venezuelan Oil Capture

I. The Definition

Economic Incentivized Tactical Warfare (EITW) is a structural doctrine of statecraft in which military force is deployed not for territorial conquest, regime change, or defense, but as a tactical mechanism to seize and control economic assets.

The military is the vector. The economy is the target. The incentive is profit.

EITW operates through the convergence of four structural vectors:

  • The Military Vector: The tactical use of armed force (special operations, airstrikes, naval blockades) to remove the obstacle and secure the asset.
  • The Economic Vector: The negotiation of a corporate agreement (often secret) that transfers control of the asset (oil, minerals, shipping corridors) to a designated partner.
  • The Financial Vector: The creation of a corporate firewall (offshore parent companies, long-term board structures, supermajority requirements) designed to prevent future leaders from dismantling the deal.
  • The Semantic Vector: The framing of the operation as law enforcement (narco-terrorism, sanctions enforcement) rather than economic seizure.

III. The Case Study: The Secret Venezuelan Oil Deal

ProPublica's review of the secret 18-page strategic partnership agreement (October 21, 2026) confirms the structural identity of EITW. The deal reveals:

1. The Partner: Alejandro Betancourt

The U.S. chose Alejandro Betancourt, a Venezuelan oligarch who has faced money-laundering investigations in three countries. Betancourt was confined to house arrest in England, fighting a Swiss extradition request, at the time of the deal. He has not been charged in any country. His company, North American Blue Energy Partners (NABEP), is the second-largest private oil firm in Venezuela.

2. The Corporate Structure: A Corporate Firewall

The deal is designed to prevent future presidents from changing it. The U.S. government took a 35% stake in NABEP by acquiring shares in a Barbados-registered parent company. The agreement safeguards Betancourt's position as CEO and establishes a seven-member board (with a U.S. citizen majority) for an initial 15-year term. The deal does not require any Venezuelan government participation on the board.

3. The Hidden Investors

Some of the Trump administration's partners could remain anonymous, hidden behind the Barbados-based parent company. The agreement provides little information about the owners and investors. The deal requires a supermajority vote to oust Betancourt or change the board composition. Under Barbados law, there is almost nothing that can force those interests to be revealed publicly.

4. The U.S. Purchasing Rights

The U.S. receives a deep discount on 20% of NABEP's oil at $30 a barrel (less than a third of recent market prices). The U.S. also has "first priority" to purchase all remaining oil production for the next 50 years, including an irrevocable right to buy the entire supply in a national emergency. A "most favored nation" clause requires Betancourt's firm to disclose other business agreements and grant Washington matching terms.

5. The Lack of Venezuelan Benefits

The agreement does not explicitly guarantee that Betancourt's company is obligated to pay the estimated $200 billion in royalties and tax payments to Venezuela that the White House claimed. The role of the Venezuelan government is largely absent from the text.

6. The Legal Jurisdiction

Venezuela cannot challenge the deal in its own courts. Any conflict must be settled under U.S. law, specifically in the United States District Court for the Northern District of Texas. Where U.S. law does not apply, Texas state law governs.

IV. The Structural Logic

EITW is not a conspiracy. It is a structural incentive. The logic is as follows:

  1. Seize the Territory: Use military force to remove the obstacle and secure the asset.
  2. Negotiate the Deal: Create a corporate agreement that transfers control to a designated partner (often with a history of legal scrutiny, ensuring loyalty and leverage).
  3. Build the Firewall: Structure the deal (offshore entities, long-term boards, supermajority requirements) to prevent future leaders from undoing it.
  4. Extract the Profit: Secure discounted purchasing rights, priority access, and hidden investor stakes.
  5. Frame the Narrative: Present the operation as law enforcement, humanitarian intervention, or national security, never as economic seizure.

The result is a public-private partnership in undeclared income extraction. The taxpayer funds the military. The military secures the asset. The corporate partner extracts the profit. The deal is classified. The public does not know the terms.
